None of these is a theorem: a wrong coefficient, a division by zero, a
condition of existence that is ignored, an inequality that holds only from
some point on, a converse that fails, a condition on all the parts of a set
that says less than it seems (it holds in every finite set), a statement on
integers that the engine cannot decide and Vampire's arithmetic refutes, a
claim that does not follow from two sets being equipotent.
None may be reported as proved.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{n} k = \frac{n(n+2)}{2}$
\item $\displaystyle \forall x \in \mathbb{R}\ \left(\frac{x}{x} = 1\right)$
\item $\displaystyle \forall x \in \mathbb{R}\ \left(\sqrt{x^{2}} = x\right)$
\item $\displaystyle 2^{n} > n^{2}$
\item $\displaystyle \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = 2$
\item $\displaystyle (A \subseteq S \land B \subseteq S \land A \subseteq B) \implies B \subseteq A$
\item $\displaystyle (f \colon S \to S \land \forall x, y \in S\ (f(x) = f(y) \implies x = y)) \implies \forall x \in S\ (f(f(x)) = x)$
\item $\displaystyle (f \colon S \to S \land \forall X, Y \subseteq S\ (f(X) \subseteq f(Y) \implies X \subseteq Y)) \implies \forall y \in S\ \exists x \in S\ (f(x) = y)$
\item $\displaystyle \forall x, y \in \mathbb{Z}\ ((\exists k \in \mathbb{Z}\ (x = k^{2} + 7) \land \exists k \in \mathbb{Z}\ (y = k^{2} + 7)) \implies x = y)$
\item $\displaystyle (S \sim T \land \forall x, y \in S\ (x = y)) \implies \forall u \in T\ (u \neq u)$
\end{enumerate}
